**Topic**: people guessing which of two TikTok videos got more views.
- **Source**: Hughes & Weninger, *Echoes in the Algorithm* ([arXiv 2609.09365](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09365)). I found no journal or conference publication, so the cards and caption label it a preprint (not yet peer-reviewed).
- **Study**: 363 US adults compared pairs of TikTok videos 3,513 times, with likes and view counts hidden. For each pair they answered two questions: which video they liked more, and which one they thought had more views.
- **Results**: they picked the more-viewed video about 57% of the time, where random guessing gets 50%. In about 83% of pairs, the video they liked was also the one they said had more views.
- **Main caveat (slide 5)**: people were always asked which video they liked before guessing views. So the 83% overlap may come from that fixed order, and it doesn't show that people naturally judge popularity by their own taste. The authors call this their biggest limitation.
- **Visual**: a balance scale with two video frames on it. I kept it level so it doesn't suggest people "weigh popularity wrongly."
